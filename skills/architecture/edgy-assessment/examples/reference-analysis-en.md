# Acme Oy — EDGY 23 Enterprise Design Analysis

**Source:** acme.example (public website, March 2026)
**Method:** EDGY 23 Enterprise Design
**Analyst:** Claude (Anthropic)

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Acme is a 30-person software consultancy founded in 2017 in Otaniemi, Finland. The company positions itself as a long-term technology partner, emphasising maintainable systems, direct communication, and European infrastructure.

The EDGY analysis reveals strong coherence between the Identity and Architecture facets, but identifies development areas in the coherence between the Experience facet and intersection elements.

---

## 2. Identity Facet — Why does Acme exist?

### Purpose
**Sustainable technology partnership** — "We build systems that someone actually wants to maintain."

Acme's purpose is not selling technology but long-term partnership. This differentiates the company from consultancies that focus on project deliveries.

### Story
**From Otaniemi to 200+ projects** — Founded in 2017 in Finland's technology hub, grown with a passionate team to 30 professionals. 141 years of combined experience.

The story is authentic and differentiating: startup-spirited growth without external funding, emphasising independence and authenticity.

### Content
**Direct and honest communication** — "Underpromise, overdeliver" philosophy. Communication even when it is uncomfortable. Blog, client references, and promises such as "we don't sell AI as a product".

**Strength:** Content expresses the purpose consistently — promises and the story speak the same language.

## 3. Architecture Facet — How does Acme operate?

### Capability
Acme offers a broad range of services:

| Capability | Nature | Level |
|------------|--------|-------|
| Software development (web/mobile) | Differentiating, in-house | Core |
| UI/UX design | In-house | Supporting |
| Cloud infrastructure (UpCloud) | Differentiating, in-house | Core |
| QA and testing | In-house | Supporting |
| DevOps and maintenance (First Line AI) | Differentiating, in-house | Core |
| Growth hacking | In-house | Supporting |

**Note:** The capability range is broad for a 30-person company. This can be both a strength (comprehensive service) and a risk (lack of focus).

### Asset
- **React/Vue/Node/TypeScript technology stack** — modern and widely adopted
- **Google Cloud + UpCloud infrastructure** — European emphasis is differentiating
- **First Line AI monitoring tool** — proprietary product that supports the maintenance service

### Process
- **Agile development** — MVP approach, iterative delivery
- **Continuous maintenance** — proactive updates and fixes with AI assistance
- **Team leasing** — embedded developers in the client's team

**Strength:** Processes realise capabilities directly — agile development maps to software development, continuous maintenance maps to DevOps.

## 4. Experience Facet — What role does Acme play in people's lives?

### Task
Core customer tasks with Acme:

1. **New application development** — the largest service area
2. **Existing service maintenance** — through the First Line service
3. **Development team scaling** — team leasing
4. **Technology consulting** — advisory services
5. **MVP/prototype building** — rapid validation (5-7k EUR)

### Channel
| Channel | Type | Role |
|---------|------|------|
| acme.example | Digital, asynchronous | First point of contact |
| Phone/WhatsApp | Digital, synchronous | Quick contact |
| Email | Digital, asynchronous | Proposals and contracts |
| LinkedIn/social media | Digital, asynchronous | Brand building |
| Otaniemi office | Physical, synchronous | In-depth discussions |

### Journey
```
Contact → Needs assessment → Design → MVP/Proto → Development → Launch → Maintenance
```

**Strength:** The customer journey covers the full lifecycle from idea to maintained service, which matches the Purpose promise of long-term partnership.

## 5. Intersection Elements — Bridges Between Facets

### Organisation (Identity ↔ Architecture)
**30-person team-based organisation** — CEO Atte Pohjanmaa, sales/marketing Timo Moilanen. Embedded team models in client projects.

- **pursues** Purpose (sustainable partnership)
- **has** capabilities (software development, UI/UX, DevOps)
- **performs** processes (agile development, maintenance)
- **builds** brand (reliable partner)

**Coherence:** Strong. The organisational structure supports both identity (team-based, non-hierarchical) and architecture (embedded teams enable agile development).

### Product (Architecture ↔ Experience)
Three product lines:

1. **Web/mobile applications** — custom-built solutions
2. **First Line maintenance service** — AI-powered monitoring and maintenance
3. **UpCloud infrastructure service** — European cloud infrastructure

- **serves** customer tasks (development, maintenance)
- **features in** various stages of the customer journey

**Coherence:** Good. Products map directly to customer tasks. First Line is particularly coherent — it connects the DevOps capability to the maintenance task.

### Brand (Identity ↔ Experience)
**"Reliable European software house"** — "fits every mould", "no bullshit posturing".

- **represents** purpose (sustainable partnership)
- **evokes** story (growth story without hype)
- **supports** customer tasks (trust reduces risk)

**Coherence:** Good. The anti-hype brand is credible for a 30-person company and resonates particularly well with Finnish B2B clients.

## 6. Strengths

1. **Purpose–Architecture coherence:** The purpose (sustainable partnership) is directly realised in the architecture (maintenance service, continuous development, First Line AI).
2. **Differentiating value proposition:** "Underpromise, overdeliver" and the anti-hype positioning differentiate from competitors.
3. **European infrastructure:** The UpCloud partnership and GDPR emphasis are a genuine differentiator.
4. **Full lifecycle service:** Few 30-person companies cover the entire chain from design to maintenance.
5. **First Line AI:** A proprietary product that concretely supports the Purpose promise.

---

## 7. Development Areas and Gaps

### 7.1 Capability focus
14 service areas in a 30-person company is broad. The EDGY analysis recommends differentiation:
- **Differentiating** (invest in): software development, DevOps/First Line, cloud infrastructure
- **Commodity** (outsource or reduce): graphic design, e-commerce, growth hacking

### 7.2 Experience facet clarity
The customer journey is implicit but not explicitly described on the website. Recommendation: visualise the customer journey more clearly in marketing materials.

### 7.3 Content strategy
The blog exists but the content strategy appears reactive. The "underpromise, overdeliver" philosophy could appear more strongly in case studies that prove the promise.

### 7.4 Brand–Experience connection
The brand "reliable partner" is strong but generic. Recommendation: concretise the brand promise in the customer experience — e.g. SLA guarantees, transparent processes, automated reports.

### 7.5 Organisation scalability
In a 30-person organisation, the embedded team model scales in a limited way. The partnership network (25+ companies) compensates for this, but does not appear in the EDGY model.

---

## 8. Recommendations

| # | Recommendation | EDGY Element | Priority |
|---|---------------|--------------|----------|
| 1 | Prioritise 3-4 core capabilities, outsource the rest | Capability | High |
| 2 | Describe the customer journey explicitly on the website | Journey | Medium |
| 3 | Create a case study series that proves "underpromise, overdeliver" | Content | Medium |
| 4 | Concretise the brand promise with measurable SLA levels | Brand | Medium |
| 5 | Model the partnership network with EDGY People/Organisation elements | Organisation | Low |
| 6 | Develop First Line into its own product brand | Product/Brand | Low |
