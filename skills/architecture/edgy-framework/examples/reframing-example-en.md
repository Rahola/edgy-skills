# EDGY Reframing: "This train is too slow"

## Original Perspective
Experience facet: Customer experience of travel time (Journey/Task)

## Expanded Analysis

### Identity
- **Purpose**: Is speed part of our purpose? Or is our purpose "to revolutionise mobility in Europe" — making speed just one means among many?
- **Story**: How do we talk about travel? Do we emphasise speed or travel quality? Is our story "the fastest route" or "the best experience"?
- **Content**: What do we communicate about travel times? Is our information realistic? Is schedule communication clear?

### Architecture
- **Capability**: Do we have the capability to improve speed (rail infrastructure, rolling stock, schedule optimisation)? Are critical capabilities missing?
- **Process**: Which processes create slowness — maintenance processes, transfer processes, track capacity management?
- **Asset**: Is the rolling stock modern enough? Is the track suitable for faster trains? What are the technical constraints?

### Experience
- **Task**: Is the customer's task "travel fast" or "arrive reliably and comfortably"?
- **Journey**: At which stage of the journey is slowness felt as problematic? Waiting? Transfers? The journey itself?
- **Channel**: In which channels do customers experience slowness — on the train, in the app (real-time information), at the station?

### Intersections
- **Organisation**: Is the organisation structured so that the speed problem is someone's responsibility? Or is it "nobody's" area?
- **Product**: Should the product portfolio include an "express" product? Or should all trains be improved?
- **Brand**: Do we want to be "the fastest" or "the most reliable"? How does slowness affect our brand image?

## Conclusion
The original challenge "this train is too slow" appears to be an Experience facet problem, but its true scope extends to:
1. **Architecture**: Rail infrastructure and rolling stock (Asset), maintenance processes (Process)
2. **Identity**: Redefining purpose and story — speed vs. reliability
3. **Organisation**: Clarifying areas of responsibility
4. **Product**: Differentiating the product portfolio by speed
